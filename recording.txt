Let me start with something we've all lived through. Every to-do list works for about a week. Then it grows into a flat wall of items where "call Mom" sits right next to "prep the quarterly numbers." Everything looks equally urgent, which means nothing does. The hard part was never remembering what to do - it's deciding what to do next, and knowing what you can safely ignore right now. A plain list can't answer that. GSD is built to.

So what exactly is GSD? GSD - Getting Stuff Done - is built on the Getting Things Done framework: get everything out of your head into a trusted system, then choose your next action by an item's attributes - priority, urgency, size, category - instead of by whatever's shouting loudest. And it's deliberately tiny: one HTML file, no install, no account, no server, works offline. This demo list belongs to Michael Scott - let's use it to get him sorted.

The first thing you'll notice is how much the list tells you at a glance. Here's the first payoff. You can read this list without reading it. The colored border and dot are priority - red High, orange Medium, green Low. The date chip is urgency: "Prep quarterly numbers" is red because it's already overdue; "Call Mom" is amber because it's due today. The solid pill is the category, the grey badge is size - effort at a glance. So before doing anything, Michael already knows the red, overdue work items are where his attention belongs - and the "Update LinkedIn to World's Best Boss" task can wait.

Now for the feature that really sets GSD apart. That instinct is exactly what Smart sort automates - and it's the heart of the app. Instead of sorting on one field, it scores every task by blending urgency, priority, and a quick-win boost for smaller tasks, then floats the important-and-urgent ones to the top. Watch: the overdue quarterly numbers and the past-due car registration rise up here; the Dundie Awards and calling Mom sit right behind; and the low-priority, no-deadline stuff sinks to the bottom on its own. That's the whole promise - you open the app and the top of the list is your answer to "what now?" No triage required.

Just as important as knowing what to do is hiding what you can't. The other half of staying focused is hiding what you can't act on. That's Awaiting - pure GTD "waiting-on." Michael's waiting on a promo rate to book Jamaica, and on HR to transfer Toby. Those don't belong in his active list, so they live down here, out of his face. Deferring anything takes one click - you note what you're waiting on and when to resurface it. And the key part: on that date it automatically returns to the active list. You never have to remember to check back - which is the entire point of a trusted system.

Of course, none of this matters if getting tasks in is a chore. Getting tasks in is just as low-friction. The plus button captures a new task with all its fields; clicking any row edits, completes, or deletes it; hovering duplicates a repeat. And search and filters shrink a long list in a keystroke. None of it needs explaining - it just stays out of your way so the framework can do the work.

Before we wrap, let's talk about where all this actually lives. One more thing that matters: you own your data. GSD stores your tasks as plain JSON files in a folder you pick - that folder is the database, and every change auto-saves there instantly. No cloud, no lock-in. You can back up to a zip in one click, move the folder to another machine, even diff it in git. It's your list, in your files, forever.

So let's bring it all together. One offline file, your data, and a list that tells you what to do next: priority and urgency visible at a glance, Smart sort surfacing what matters, and everything you're just waiting on quietly out of the way until it's actionable. Get it out of your head. Let GSD tell you what's next. That's getting stuff done.
