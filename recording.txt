Every to-do list becomes a flat wall where "call Mom" sits next to "prep the quarterly numbers" - everything looks urgent, so nothing is. GSD fixes that. Built on the Getting Things Done framework, it's one offline HTML file - no install, no account, no server - that picks your next action by an item's attributes instead of by whatever's shouting loudest. This is Michael Scott's list; let's put it to work.

Start with what the list tells you before you touch it. The colored border and dot are priority - red High, orange Medium, green Low. The date chip is urgency - red for overdue like "Prep quarterly numbers," amber for due today like "Call Mom." The solid pill is the category, the grey badge is size. One glance and Michael knows the red, overdue work is where his attention goes - and "Update LinkedIn to World's Best Boss" can wait.

Smart sort turns that instinct into the default, and it's the heart of the app. Instead of sorting on one field, it scores every task by blending urgency, priority, and a quick-win boost for smaller tasks, then floats the important-and-urgent ones to the top. The overdue quarterly numbers and the past-due car registration rise up; the Dundie Awards and calling Mom sit right behind; the low-priority, no-deadline stuff sinks to the bottom on its own. You open the app and the top of the list is your answer to "what now?" - no triage required.

Focus is also about hiding what you can't act on. That's Awaiting - pure GTD "waiting-on." Michael's waiting on a promo rate to book Jamaica, and on HR to transfer Toby - those live down here, out of his active list. Deferring takes one click: you note what you're waiting on and a bring-back date, and on that date it automatically returns to the active list. You never have to remember to check back.

Not everything is a task yet. The Idea Pipeline is your "someday/maybe" bucket - ideas with no due date and no done state, so they never clutter what's actionable. It's where Michael parks the big swings: a family-friendly restaurant chain, Michael Scott Paper Company 2.0, and his book, "Somehow I Manage." Capture one with "+ Add idea," or hover any task and hit the lightbulb to park it for later. When an idea's time comes, one click promotes it back to the to-do list. Your ambitions stay captured without drowning today's work.

Getting things in stays effortless. The plus button captures a task with all its fields; clicking a row edits, completes, or deletes it; hovering duplicates a repeat. Search and filters shrink a long list in a keystroke. It just stays out of your way so the framework can do the work.

And you own all of it. GSD stores your tasks as plain JSON files in a folder you pick - that folder is the database, and every change auto-saves there instantly. No cloud, no lock-in. Back up to a zip in one click, move the folder to another machine, even diff it in git. It's your list, in your files, forever.

One offline file, your data, and a list that tells you what to do next: priority and urgency visible at a glance, Smart sort surfacing what matters, Awaiting holding what you can't act on, and the Idea Pipeline keeping someday alive without crowding today. Get it out of your head. Let GSD tell you what's next. That's getting stuff done.
